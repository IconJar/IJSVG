Filter pipeline experiments — 2026-10-01
Apple M1 Max; macOS 27.0.1 (26A434); Xcode Debug framework.

Run after building with Xcode:
  sh Benchmarks/run.sh /path/to/Build/Products/Debug
Requires access to Metal; a sandbox with no Metal device cannot benchmark this.
The script compiles standalone harnesses, leaving application targets unchanged.

Batch preflight
The existing transform-aware preflight now estimates collection bitmap storage,
RGBA8 replay storage with at most 2x atlas packing overhead, the largest source,
one megapixel of pending sources, a 44-byte/pixel Metal lease, and parameter
headroom. Admission budget: 128 MiB; retained output cap: 8 megapixels.
Pending work still flushes at one megapixel. Atlas packing recursively splits
whole images to preserve convolution neighbors and bound padding.
This is an estimate, not a process memory ceiling: CPU filter intermediates,
Core Image caches, and simultaneous callers can consume additional memory.

The 2x car now submits five multi-job blur batches per draw. The original
four-megapixel retention cap rejected it. Timings are local observations,
not cross-device guarantees. CarBatch.m also writes PNG and raw RGBA outputs.

Wide blur
FilterPipeline.m compares full resolution CI against high-quality half/quarter
resampling, scaled-radius Gaussian blur, then bilinear upsampling. Fresh input
identities prevent cached-output timings. Three warmups and fifteen alternating
samples; sizes 128/512/1024; radii 6/12/24; sRGB and linearRGB; translucent edges,
fractional crops and thin high-contrast lines. Output is premultiplied sRGB RGBA8.
See blur-results.csv for timings and max/mean absolute channel differences.
At 1024, half-resolution is commonly 10–20% faster, but differences reach
11/255 (quarter: 24/255). Even radius 24 reaches 3/255 at half resolution.
Decision: do not enable downsampling; it exceeds the existing 2/255 tolerance.
Preserved the pre-existing direct Metal radius-12 extension. Expanded its
reference comparisons through radius 12 in both spaces and orientations;
unsupported-radius checks now cover 12.1 and 24.

Filter chains
OpacityChain.m compares six opacity-only matrix/clamp stages with fractional
crops followed by source-over compositing, with/without the redundant RGB
conversion pairs. Four warmups, thirty alternating samples, fresh input data.
See opacity-results.csv. sRGB medians improve approximately 10–14%, with zero
RGBA8 differences. LinearRGB benefit is inconsistent. The production change
removes those conversion pairs only for identity RGB plus alpha scale in [0,1].
Clamps, primitive crops, named results and compositing order remain intact.
General matrix/composite fusion is intentionally not enabled: removing stage
clamps or crossing color-space/crop boundaries can change SVG semantics.

Validation
Xcode build passes. All 23 selected batch, concurrency, filter-matrix and Metal
blur tests pass across targeted runs. Updated large-batch coverage includes
6, 20 and 40 filters (chunking, admitted larger output, over-budget fallback).

Follow-up: compatible filter chains
----------------------------------
Run: sh Benchmarks/run-chains.sh /path/to/Build/Products/Debug

The car contains only single Gaussian-blur filters. The mixed/complex corpus
profile is saved in filter-chain-profile.csv. The supplied health-worker SVG
adds 16 flood/blend/blur chains and seven inner-shadow variants. Its two double
inner-shadow filters were among the largest inclusive filter costs. Inclusive
profiles include cold calls, nested source drawing and collection overhead;
they are not isolated GPU timings.

Implemented a narrow simplification in IJSVGFilterGraph: normal blending of
SourceGraphic over an explicitly named, zero-opacity flood is an identity when
both filter and primitive crops are pixel-aligned and the blend uses sRGB.
The compositor and its color conversions are omitted; primitive-region
calculation, crop, named-result retention and later operations are preserved.
Reserved source-name collisions, nonnormal blends, nontransparent floods,
fractional crops and linearRGB retain the previous evaluator.

Why the guards matter: CI can coalesce adjacent crops when the compositor is
removed. Fractional crops accumulate edge coverage, unlike integral crops.
Repeated linearRGB compositions also accumulated a 3/255 rounding difference
when their intermediate conversion was removed, so those are excluded.
A separate tint/atop fusion experiment was discarded: inconsistent speed gains
and a concurrent reference comparison outside the existing 2/255 tolerance.

Final repeated color/opacity/compositing benchmark: 24 overlapping filters,
three warmups, 21 timed samples per variant, alternating order in one process,
fresh SVG each draw, parsing excluded. Apple M1 Max, Debug framework.
See color-chain-results.csv for the complete measurements.
  sRGB size    reference ms    optimized ms    max RGBA8 difference
  32              7.165           5.111             0
  64              9.416           6.474             1
  128            10.915           9.183             1
  256            22.223          21.459             1
This synthetic workload improves 16–31% at 32–128 pixels; it is not a claim
about every SVG. linearRGB selects the original path and matches exactly.

health-worker-color-light.svg is included as a regression/benchmark fixture.
It matches reference pixels exactly at 32,64,128,256,512,1024. Its relevant
crops are fractional, so this optimization deliberately does not apply; no
speedup is claimed for that asset. See health-worker-chain-results.csv.

Validation: Xcode build passes and all 33 selected inner-shadow, Metal-shadow,
filter-matrix and concurrency tests pass. The standalone comparison additionally
checked 120 size/recipe combinations (two color spaces, orientations, explicit
regions, named branches, opaque floods, multiply blends and source collisions).
The final sRGB-only guard is further verified by the eight alternating color
chain comparisons, including zero optimized operations in linearRGB cases.
